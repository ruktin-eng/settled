The Life in the UK test: a complete guide

Last checked October 2026

The Life in the UK test checks that you know about British traditions, history, government and everyday life. Here is what you need to know before you book.

Booking rules and fees can change. Always check the details on GOV.UK before you book.

Who needs to take it

Most people aged 18 to 64 need to pass the test when they apply for:

  • indefinite leave to remain (settlement) in the UK
  • British citizenship by naturalisation

You usually don’t need to take it if you are under 18 or 65 or over, or if you have a long-term physical or mental condition that makes it hard to take. If you have passed it before, you don’t need to take it again: you use the same pass reference number.

What the test looks like

24multiple-choice questions
45minutes to answer them
18correct answers needed

The test is taken on a computer at an approved test centre, in English. The questions are based on the official handbook, Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents (third edition). Some questions ask you to pick one answer, some ask you to pick two, and some ask whether a statement is true or false.

You need to get 75% of the questions right to pass. That is 18 out of 24.

How to book

  • Book online through GOV.UK. You can only book the test there, and you can’t book it by phone or at a test centre.
  • You need an email address, a valid form of ID and a debit or credit card to pay the fee.
  • Book at least 3 days ahead. There are around 30 test centres across the UK.
  • The ID you bring on the day must be the same ID you used to book.

On the day

  • Arrive in good time and bring the ID you booked with.
  • You may be asked to confirm your address. Check the booking email for what to bring.
  • Phones and notes aren’t allowed in the test room.
  • You get your result at the test centre straight after the test.

If you pass, or if you don’t

If you pass, you get a unique reference number. You need it for your settlement or citizenship application, so keep it safe.

If you fail, you can book again. You must wait at least 7 days, and you pay the fee again each time. There is no limit on how many times you can take it.

How to prepare

  1. Read the handbook. Every question comes from it. Our chapter summaries show what each chapter covers.
  2. Practise a little every day. Short daily sessions work better than one long cram. Try our free practice test.
  3. Learn from your mistakes. Read the explanation whenever you get a question wrong. The wrong options are often close to the right one.
  4. Know your dates and numbers. Many questions ask for a year, a number or a name. History is the largest chapter.
  5. Sit full mock tests. When you can score 21 or more out of 24 again and again, you’re likely ready.

The Settled app has over 1,200 practice questions and timed mock tests, if you want more practice.
